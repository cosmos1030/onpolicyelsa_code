## 1. Verdict: STE does not help at 2:4 in this framework

The 2:4 line of work was run AST-style (`--gmp_ste`, thu-ml/Adaptive-Sparse-
Trainer, AAAI 2025, cloned at `Adaptive-Sparse-Trainer/`). It loses.

| arm (4B 2:4, Fisher, d=0.01, lr 1e-4) | late flips / PGD event | avg5 |
|---|---|---|
| **no-STE control** | **532** | **47.23** |
| STE + decay 2e-3 | 24,594 | **45.68** ← best STE |
| STE + decay 1e-3 | 31,411 | 44.86 / 44.54 (2 seeds) |
| STE + decay 3e-3 | 22,801 | 43.46 |
| STE + decay 5e-3 | 18,236 | 43.52 |
| STE + shrink 1e-4 / 5e-4 | ~1.0–1.2M | 42.14 (invalid, see §3) |
| plain STE (decay 0) | 1,096,098 | 40.55 / 41.67 (2 seeds) |
| STE + decay 1e-4 | — | 8.66 (collapse) |

Seed spread at d=0.01 decay 1e-3 is 0.32, so these gaps are not noise.

**Why it loses.** A masked weight's straight-through gradient is the gradient it
would have if it were active, evaluated where it contributes nothing to the
forward, so nothing validates it and it never self-corrects. Measured on the
step-1792 checkpoint: dead coordinates drifted 2–3x further from init than live
ones (0.068–0.092 vs 0.020–0.057 of the layer's mean |w|). Their saliency
`F·w²` keeps climbing, and since the 2:4 group's 3rd/2nd saliency margin is
tight (median 0.41, 7.5% of groups above 0.9) the projection keeps flipping
them. The control converges — its last projection changes 0 coordinates — while
plain STE still flips 1.1M. Train loss barely notices (0.5069 vs 0.4992)
because it is always measured under the mask currently in force; the cost shows
up only at eval.

**Why more damping does not fix it.** Churn falls monotonically with decay
(1e-3 → 5e-3: 31K → 18K) but accuracy peaks at 2e-3 and falls after. Past that
the decay pins dead weights at zero and revival — STE's entire purpose — stops,
leaving "no-STE plus noise". The optimum at 2e-3 is exactly where AST's total
decay pressure lands once rescaled (AST: 40,000 steps × 1e-4; ours: 2,048 × 2e-3).

**The structural reason, which no tuning reaches.** In AST, STE replaces a
FROZEN mask, so revival is pure upside. Our baseline already revives, through
the KL-gated projection. STE adds a second revival path that no forward pass
validates, on top of one that works. Remaining differences to AST (SLoRB's extra
parameters, KD at T=2 weighted 2:1 over the task loss, and ~400x the token
budget — AST's LLaMA recipe is `max_duration=15000ba` × batch 128 × seqlen 4096
= 50B tokens vs our 0.13B) would all have to be closed before AST's numbers are
comparable, and none of them touch this point.

**Do not re-litigate the 1.7B STE runs as evidence.** Matched against their own
non-STE siblings they were a wash (S50 44.50 vs 44.20, S60 34.04 vs 34.46, S70
23.09 vs 24.18). What collapsed there was the `gmp_pgd_max_swap_frac` cap sweep
(−3.9 to −8.6), and that flag is ignored whenever `gmp_pgd_kl_budget > 0`, i.e.
in every current run.

## 2. `--gmp_ste_shrink` — the Fisher-clean variant, and why it did nothing

AST puts its decay on the gradient, which is coherent for AST because AST ranks
by |w| / wanda. It is not coherent here: our saliency is `F·w²` with F = Adam's
`exp_avg_sq`, so a gradient-side decay is itself accumulated into F and pushes
up the Fisher of the coordinates it is trying to suppress. `--gmp_ste_shrink`
applies `w ← w(1 − ρ_t)` to dead coordinates AFTER `optimizer.step()` instead,
so F stays clean. At ρ = 1e-4 / 5e-4 it did not damp churn at all (~1.0–1.2M):
shrinking `w²` alone does not reorder the ranking while F is untouched. The flag
is kept; ρ is a direct per-step rate and is deliberately not scaled by lr.

## 3. A save-path collision destroyed two runs — fixed

`{_run_tag(FLAGS)}_{ts}` is not unique. `_run_tag` carries only sparsity, lr and
the OPKD lambda, so every arm of a sweep that varies anything else produces the
SAME tag, and `ts` has one-second resolution. Arms launched 20s apart run in
lockstep and reach the save within the same second.

`ast24_shrink1e-4` and `ast24_shrink5e-4` both wrote
`models/gmp_s50pct_lr0.0001_onpol_lmda0.33_20260911_192839`. One overwrote the
other's weights and BOTH evaluated the survivor, reporting byte-identical scores
on all five benchmarks (63.40/25.76/36.60/9.33/75.59) despite different training
losses (0.4538 vs 0.4545). Both are discarded — which of them the surviving
weights belonged to cannot be recovered.

**An audit of all 115 runs that ever saved a model found this to be the only
collision.** No earlier result is affected. The path now carries the pid.

Their step-1792 checkpoints DO survive separately, in per-arm `CKPT_DIR`s, so
both can still be scored with `b200_scripts/resume_eval_lighteval.sh` without
retraining (~40 min each) if the shrink numbers are ever wanted.

* **8B S70 d=0.03 = 50.25**, the new S70 optimum (grid: 0.01 49.84, 0.02 50.12,
  0.03 50.25, 0.05 49.11). The last hole that could move the S70 headline.

  confirms the tok=256 baseline (49.31) was under-resourced, so SCOUT's 4B 2:4
  deficit widens from −2.08 to **−2.92**.
* `supervisor_v3.sh` replaces the `queue_*.sh` pattern: it re-reads
  `sweep_queue.tsv` every 120s (add a row, it starts at the next free GPU, no
  restart), resumes dead jobs from their newest checkpoint, fills every free GPU
  per cycle, and writes an IDLE ALARM when the queue runs dry. The old queues
  exited after their last launch, which is how 4 GPUs sat idle 4.5 hours on
  2026-09-10 after an OOM.
